Objection 1 · Fixed duration

“Acalabrutinib plus venetoclax is an appropriate option for my treatment-naive patients — it has good efficacy and it is fixed duration.”

Response

Acknowledge: “Absolutely, fixed-duration combinations are an important option, and venetoclax-based regimens have strong efficacy.”

“In the same time it is critical to anchor treatment choice on the strongest frontline BTKi evidence, especially for long-term disease control and diverse patient profiles.”

SEQUOIA Arm A – Frontline CLL: Zanubrutinib demonstrated superior PFS vs BR. Effective in TN patients with and without del(17p). Confirms Zanubrutinib as a robust frontline standard.

SEQUOIA Arm C – High-Risk CLL: 6-year durable disease control in del(17p)/TP53-mutated patients. This is the hardest-to-treat population, where depth and durability matter most.

Key messageZanubrutinib delivers proven long-term control, not just short-term response.
Supporting data points
  • AMPLIFY was studied in a highly preselected patient population that excluded high-risk features and is not representative of the real-world CLL patient population.
  • Median age of patients in the AMPLIFY trial was 61 years (versus the median age at CLL diagnosis is 72 years old).
  • 73.2% of patients in the AMPLIFY trial were ≤65 years old.
  • All patients in the AMPLIFY trial had a low comorbidity burden: All patients had a CIRS-Geriatric score of ≤6 and 91.8% had an ECOG score of 0 or 1.
  • Excluded patients with del(17p)/TP53 mutations (account for ~15% of all patients).
  • 36-month PFS: AV 76.5%; AVO 83.1%; FCR/BR 66.5%.
  • CR/CRi: AV 8.9%; AVO 15.7%; FCR/BR 5.5%.
  • uMRD: AV 26.8%; AVO 66.4%; FCR/BR 51.0%.
  • AMPLIFY excluded patients with del(17p)/TP53, therefore there is no evidence to suggest AV or AVO are efficacious in this population.

Objection 4 · Fixed duration

“Ibrutinib plus venetoclax is the best option for my young patients who want deep remission and time off treatment. We have strong data from GLOW and CAPTIVATE.”

Response

Acknowledge: “You’re absolutely right — GLOW and CAPTIVATE showed that Ibrutinib + Venetoclax can achieve deep remissions and MRD negativity, especially in younger patients. However, being young does not automatically mean a patient needs intensive combination therapy.”

Clinical Reality – Overtreatment Risk in Young Patients: Many young CLL patients have favorable biology and indolent disease course; can achieve long-term disease control with BTKi monotherapy. Using combination therapy upfront may expose them to unnecessary toxicity without added survival benefit.

BRUKINSA is the only BTKi with superior and sustained PFS vs ibrutinib in a H2H trial in R/R CLL and demonstrates superior PFS vs BR in TN patients. This clinical benefit is consistent across patient subgroups, regardless of age, mutation status, including unmutated IGHV or genetic risk factors, such as del(17p) or TP53 mutations, providing confidence in its broad and durable efficacy.

BRUKINSA provides unmatched dosing flexibility. It’s the only BTKi that allows both once-daily and twice-daily dosing at home with no complicated ramp up or need for TLS monitoring, giving you the ability to tailor treatment schedules to each patient’s lifestyle and medical needs.

Supporting data points
  • GLOW, Ven+I (n=106) vs Clb+O (n=105), 46.0 months mFU — All treated patients: 42-month PFS: 74.6% vs 24.8% (HR: 0.214; 95% CI, 0.138, 0.334; p<0.0001).
  • CAPTIVATE, fixed dose Ven+I (n=159, single-arm cohort with no comparator), 49.8 months mFU — All treated patients: 4-year PFS: 79% (95% CI: 71, 84); IGHV unmutated: 4-year PFS: 73% (95% CI: 62, 81); Del(17p)/TP53 mutated: 4-year PFS: 63% (95% CI: 41, 79).
  • SEQUOIA Cohort 1 (patients without del[17p]), BRUKINSA (n=241) vs BR (n=238), 43.7 months mFU — All cohort 1 42-month PFS: 82.4% vs 50.0% (HR: 0.30; 95% CI: 0.21, 0.43; p<0.0001); IGHV mutated (HR: 0.35; 95% CI: 0.19, 0.64; p=0.00033); IGHV unmutated (HR: 0.23; 95% CI: 0.14, 0.37; p<0.0001).
  • SEQUOIA Cohort 2 (del[17p] patients only, single-arm, n=111), 47.9 months mFU — All cohort 2 42-month PFS: 79.4%.
  • ALPINE, BRUKINSA (n=327) vs ibrutinib (n=325), 39.0 months mFU — All treated patients: 36-month PFS: 64.9% vs 54.8% (HR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.53, 0.86; 2-sided descriptive p=0.0011).
  • The approved slide states plainly: “No head-to-head comparison data is available.”

Objection 5 · Fixed duration

“AV gives durable PFS without keeping the patient on long-term treatment.”

Response

BeOne lens: The 36-month PFS rate for AV (76.5%) is similar to that achieved over 72 months with BRUKINSA (74%); with nearly one-quarter of patients progressing by 36 months, this high rate of progression casts doubt on the suitability of AV as a long-term treatment option.

AV does not fulfill the potential of a ‘fixed-duration regimen’ as the response is neither deep nor durable, raising concerns around the risk for rapid relapse. Nearly a quarter of patients had relapsed or died after 36 months. AV only achieved a uMRD rate of 26.8% and a CR rate of 8.9%.

No significant OS benefit vs. FCR/BR (after adjusting for multiplicity) and offers no compelling evidence of superiority versus current SOC options, such as BTKi monotherapy.

HCP prompts: What concerns do you have about the risk of relapse when you stop fixed-duration therapy? What is the treatment goal in 1L CLL? What length of clinical follow-up do you think is appropriate to properly assess the efficacy profile of a treatment in CLL?

Objection 10 · Efficacy

“The AMPLIFY results apply to the patients I see. Why would I not generalise them?”

Response

BeOne lens: Because AMPLIFY’s patient population does not reflect real-world patients with CLL, its outcomes are not generalizable.

The median age of patients enrolled in AMPLIFY was 61 years, but the median age of CLL diagnosis is 70 years, with treatment typically initiating between the ages of ~72 and 78 years.

73.2% of patients enrolled in AMPLIFY were aged <65 years, but only 31% of newly diagnosed CLL patients are aged <65 years.

Patients with del(17p) or TP53 mutation were excluded, which accounts for ~15% of newly diagnosed patients. Patients aged ≥65 years were excluded if they had a total score of >6 on the Cumulative Illness Rating Scale for Geriatrics.

HCP prompts: How do you approach choice of therapy for patients with high-risk features? When trials more accurately mirror your clinical practice, how does that impact your interpretation of the results?

Key messageBRUKINSA has been studied in a diverse patient population, reflective of real-world patients, including patients with poor prognostic features.
Supporting data points
  • AMPLIFY is a Phase 3, multicenter, open-label, randomized trial of adults aged ≥18 years with previously untreated CLL, ECOG performance-status score of 0–2 who did not have del(17p) or TP53 mutation.
  • SEQUOIA enrolled a broad patient population that reflected the diversity of real-world populations. Cohort 1 (without del[17p]) treated with BRUKINSA: median age 70 years (81% aged ≥65 years); 54% had an ECOG PS score of 1 or 2.
